# QIRA — Worked Example (condensed)

**Companion to**: `qira-assessor` SKILL.md. Read to calibrate voice, depth, and how routing decisions get documented — not as a copy-paste template.

**Source**: *QIRA User Manual*, Annex 2, condensed and paraphrased from the dengue-in-a-fictional-Member-State scenario ("Tripura," per the manual's own disclaimer that identifying details were altered from the real event that inspired it).

---

## Scenario, condensed

A dengue outbreak has been running roughly five months, with case counts and the case-fatality rate both up substantially year-over-year, a shift in dominant serotype associated with higher hospitalization and fatality, and geographic spread beyond the capital into most districts. Roughly a third of cases are in children, with a markedly higher CFR in young children than in the overall population. The health system is under strain, with hospitalizations mounting; vector indices point to active, ongoing mosquito breeding; the country has managed dengue outbreaks before and has existing response infrastructure, but no dengue vaccine is available locally.

## How the walk resolves

| Domain | Answer | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1. High-threat hazard | NO | Dengue is not on the predefined high-threat list for this context; proceed through all domains. |
| 2. Exposure | YES | Hazard (vector) present, people in frequent contact with breeding sites, population susceptible given the serotype shift. |
| 3. Severity | YES | Elevated CFR, especially in children; documented severe morbidity. |
| 4.2 Spread and scale | YES | High natural spread potential (vector lifecycle, resistant to common insecticides), frequent contact (high container index, unplanned urbanization), geographic spread already observed. |
| 5.1 Health system overwhelmed | YES | Hospitalizations already straining capacity locally. |
| 5.2 Capacities in place | YES | Public health measures (vector control, surveillance) active; clinical management experience and capacity exist and can scale; RCCE materials ready, though awareness of the *specific new* risk factors (serotype shift, resistance) is less certain; no major disqualifying vulnerability identified. |

**Resulting risk level**: High (per the routing table: Q2=YES → Q3 → Q4.2=YES → Q5.1=YES → Q5.2=YES → **High**).

**Confidence**: Moderate — surveillance and prior-outbreak experience ground most of the assessment, but some open questions remain (e.g., how much the serotype shift and insecticide resistance are understood by the affected population).

**Proposed actions** (illustrative, drawn loosely from the manual's own example): activate the incident management structure; intensify vector control with insecticide-resistance-aware product selection; scale hospital bed capacity, particularly paediatric; target RCCE at the specific new risk factors; maintain international communication given tourism/travel interest; schedule reassessment on the next material epidemiological change.

## What this example is useful for

- Shows that **Question 4.1 is genuinely skipped** here — it only applies when Domain 2 resolves NO, and this scenario has ongoing exposure.
- Shows the **Domain 3 → 4.2 → 5.1 → 5.2** chain when severity and spread are both concerning: capacity gets checked twice (health-system-overwhelm, then broader prevention/control capacity), not just once.
- Illustrates that a **High** result can coexist with meaningful existing capacity — High doesn't mean "no capacity," it means the combination of exposure, severity, and spread outpaces what current capacity can fully absorb.
